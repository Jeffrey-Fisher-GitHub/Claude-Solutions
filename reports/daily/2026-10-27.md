## 4. Academic articles

- **Large Language Models Extend Known Research But Do Not Create New Research Directions** — [arXiv:2610.17439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17439). Using 26.9M papers and about 524K LLM hypotheses, the authors find LLM ideas are farther from existing work than human papers but have 15–23% lower "orthogonal novelty" (new direction). Their conclusion: models extend the frontier, humans still open new directions.
- **Language Models as AI Research World Models** — [arXiv:2610.12235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12235). LMs shown logs of past experiments can predict outcomes of new interventions; cross-environment knowledge cut selection regret by 78% in one setting. Adding research knowledge helped more than swapping models or raising reasoning effort.

## 5. Java and better code

- **JDK 27 is generally available (nine JEPs)** — [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4202901/jdk-27-the-new-features-of-java-27.html). Includes G1 as default GC (JEP 523), post-quantum hybrid key exchange for TLS 1.3 (527), compact object headers by default (534), plus ongoing previews: lazy constants, primitive patterns in `switch`, structured concurrency (seventh preview), and the Vector API.
- **Performance update for JDK 27** — [Inside.java](https://inside.java/2026/09/28/performance-update-jdk27/). Compact headers shrink object headers from 12 to 8 bytes (up to 22% lower heap in one benchmark), faster `HashMap` bulk copies and `URL.toString()`, and crypto speedups. The article stresses results are workload-specific and recommends changing one flag at a time when measuring.
- **Official JDK 27 project page** — [OpenJDK](https://openjdk.org/projects/jdk/27/). Authoritative JEP list and schedule for reference.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: Break the agent (safely).** Using a sandboxed toy "coding agent" (or a scripted simulation), students plant a malicious AGENTS.md-style instruction file and observe how an agent follows it, then write mitigations (allow-lists, human approval, least privilege). Ties to the OpenAI sandbox-escape and instruction-file news and to NIST AI RMF concepts.
2. **Lecture + debate: Who is liable?** Use the Texas v. OpenAI, Rhode Island v. Meta, Presley v. Anthropic and Italy's algorithmic-dismissal rule as cases. Small groups map each to a NIST AI RMF function and argue for or against the claim, practicing the line between allegation and finding.
3. **Lab: Does bigger always mean better? Measure it in Java.** Students benchmark `HashMap` bulk copies or object-heavy workloads on JDK 27 with and without `-XX:-UseCompactObjectHeaders` using JMH, then write up one-variable-at-a-time testing, linking to the novelty paper's lesson about distinguishing "farther" from "genuinely new" through careful measurement.
