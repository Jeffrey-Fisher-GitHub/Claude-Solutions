## 4. Academic articles on AI

**Worldviews in Large Language Models** (Lopez-Sola, Chandaria, Laukkonen; Nov 17) - [arXiv 2611.22670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22670). Surveys eight models with human worldview questionnaires and finds shared, somewhat bleak outlooks that the authors attribute mainly to post-training. System prompts and model-to-model dialogue shift these views.

**Large Language Models for AI Infrastructure** (Shen, Huang, Dong; Nov 14) - [arXiv 2611.19061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19061). A survey of LLMs used to design, operate and optimize training and serving infrastructure. It notes thin evidence when moving from simulators to real clusters.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: Probe an LLM's "worldview."** Following the worldviews paper, students run the same short attitude questionnaire against a local or hosted model under three different system prompts, then chart how responses shift. Discussion: post-training, alignment and why prompts are not neutral.
2. **Lecture and debate: Who governs AI agents?** Use the California v. OpenAI complaint, the governors' principles and the federal preemption bill as a case study. Teams argue for state, federal or hybrid oversight, citing the NIST AI RMF already in the course materials.
3. **Lab: Review the reviewer.** Students write a small Java class with deliberate bugs, have an AI tool review it (as in Claude Ship), then compare against their own review and unit tests. Pair with a short exercise on the Java 27 default changes (G1, compact headers) to show why automated review still needs human judgment.

*Bonus discussion: use the Nvidia and Anthropic moves to talk about hype cycles, overfitting expectations to a narrative, and what "AI bubble" claims would need as evidence.*
