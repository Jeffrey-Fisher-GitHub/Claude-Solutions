## 2. AI regulation and policy

**Midterms shift Congress** - [Tech Policy Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/democrats-target-ai-data-centers-elon-musk-and-big-tech-in-midterm-victory-speeches) (single-source)
Democrats reportedly won both chambers on Nov 3, and victory speeches targeted AI accountability and data-center impacts. Expect more oversight pressure on labs and infrastructure builders.

**EU AI Act: Dec 2, 2026 deadlines** - [Cubbbix](https://cubbbix.com/blog/ai-regulation-november-2026-global-update)
New prohibitions on AI generating non-consensual intimate imagery or CSAM, and the end of a marking grace period for older generative systems, take effect Dec 2. The AI Office also sent its first formal information requests to general-purpose AI providers on Oct 14.

**EU Digital Omnibus delays high-risk rules** - [Mean.ceo](https://blog.mean.ceo/eu-ai-act-news-november-2026/)
Standalone high-risk obligations slip to Dec 2027 and product-embedded ones to Aug 2028, while bans (since Feb 2025) and Article 50 transparency duties (since Aug 2026) already apply. Penalties go up to EUR 35M or 7% of turnover.

**US state and federal picture** - [Tech Policy Press roundup](https://www.techpolicy.press/october-2026-us-tech-policy-roundup)
California SB 53 is in force; Colorado's original AI Act was repealed in May with an uncertain replacement. NIST/NSA/CISA published voluntary practices for AI-agent incident management, and Anthropic's confidential draft federal bill would preempt some state laws (single-source).

**EU action on algorithmic design; EU-Canada alignment** - [EC digital strategy news](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news)
The Commission preliminarily found TikTok's recommendation feed in breach of the DSA, its first action on algorithmic design. Separately, the EU and Canada agreed to mutually recognize AI safety assessments.

**China draft AI export controls** - [Cubbbix](https://cubbbix.com/blog/ai-regulation-november-2026-global-update)
A draft covering 23 technologies is open for consultation until Nov 15 and is not yet law.

## 4. Academic articles on AI

**"AI Isn't Getting Dumber, We're Getting Pickier"** - [arXiv:2611.14333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14333)
Argues that perceived LLM decline on Chatbot Arena reflects stricter human raters rather than worse models.

**"Leaderboard Hacking"** - [arXiv:2611.14769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14769)
Shows preference-based arena leaderboards can be manipulated, shifting rankings by dozens of places.

**"When Should Agents Remember?"** - [arXiv:2611.14326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14326)
Proposes keeping only agent lessons that pass a falsification check, to avoid memory-driven degradation. A companion paper, ["Cleaning an Agent's History Hurts When the World Still Holds the Error"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14654), shows editing an agent's history can backfire.

**"Medical Image Segmentation: Foundation Model Adaptation versus Task-Specific Training"** - [arXiv:2611.14519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14519)
Finds task-specific models stay competitive when data is scarce or shifted.

**Huawei MACaQ 4-bit quantizer** - [arXiv:2611.10649](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.10649)
Claims quantizing a 122B-parameter model in about an hour on a single modest GPU (vendor claim).

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Leaderboard-manipulation lab.** Using the "Leaderboard Hacking" paper, students simulate a Bradley-Terry/Elo rating from pairwise votes in Python, then inject biased votes and measure rank shifts. Discussion: why benchmark scores and "models getting dumber" claims need skepticism.
2. **Agent memory design workshop.** Combine the "When Should Agents Remember?" and "Cleaning an Agent's History" papers: teams build a toy agent with a memory log and test policies (keep all, verify-then-keep, prune) in a small environment with persistent errors. Ties to reinforcement-learning ideas from the Sutton readings.
3. **Regulation-to-requirements exercise (with a Java twist).** Given the EU AI Act Dec 2 deadlines and Article 50 transparency rules, students classify sample AI systems by risk tier and write a small Java program (using JDK 25/27 records and pattern matching) that encodes the rules and flags compliance gaps, with JUnit tests. Optionally add a market-news segment on Nvidia's earnings as a case study in AI-infrastructure concentration risk.
