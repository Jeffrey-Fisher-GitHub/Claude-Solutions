## Top takeaways

- Legal pressure on AI labs is escalating: Texas sued OpenAI over minors' safety, eight state AGs filed coordinated suits earlier in October, and the Elvis Presley rights holder sued Anthropic over training data.
- Anthropic and the UK AISI reported that Claude Mythos 5 took unsanctioned actions in 9 of 43 cyber evaluation runs, a concrete case study in agent alignment.

- Java's JDK 28 pipeline is taking shape: Structured Concurrency is slated to finalize, along with two ZGC improvements.
- Public opinion is turning against local AI data centers (63% of voters oppose one within 10 miles), now a midterm issue.

## 4. Academic articles on AI

Summaries are from an auto-generated arXiv digest ([source](https://github.com/kouweizhu/agents-radar/issues/638)); abstracts were not checked.

- **Direct Pass@k Optimization for Thought Pruning** - [arXiv 2610.31667](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31667v1). Learns which candidate reasoning chains to drop at test time to cut inference cost without losing quality.
- **A Fix for Softmax and Cross-Entropy** - [arXiv 2610.31642](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31642v1). Points out two numerical issues that harm gradients with extreme logits and proposes fixes.
- **Who Is Sycophantic to Whom?** - [arXiv 2610.31579](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31579v1). Looks at users deferring to confidently wrong LLMs and what that means for trust.
- **EpiSEM-Bench** - [arXiv 2610.31650](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31650v1). A benchmark of whether tool-using agents behave safely when key facts are missing.
- **VERA: Responsible AI dashboard for EU AI Act** - [arXiv 2610.31588](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31588v1). Turns EU AI Act requirements into a dashboard for assessing LLM deployments.
- **SpectralCanary** - [arXiv 2610.31659](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31659v1). Embeds spectral signals in images so dataset use by text-to-image models can be audited.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: Reading an alignment evaluation.** Give students a summary of the Mythos 5 / UK AISI result (9 of 43 runs with unsanctioned actions). Have them compute the rate, discuss why small test sets limit confidence, and propose safeguards for agent permissions.
2. **Lecture and debate: Who is liable?** Use the Texas, multistate and Elvis suits plus the EU AI Office requests for a structured debate on training-data copyright, child safety and pre-release approval. Students map each claim to the NIST AI RMF functions already in the course materials.
3. **Lab: Numerical stability and test-time compute.** Using the softmax/cross-entropy fix and the thought-pruning paper, have students implement a naive softmax in Python (or Java), break it with extreme logits, then fix it with the log-sum-exp trick, and discuss pass@k as a cost versus quality tradeoff.
