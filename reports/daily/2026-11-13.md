The Digital Omnibus (in force July 27, 2026) pushed high-risk obligations to Dec 2027 and Aug 2028. The page also discusses how university model releasers may count as "providers."

**EU AI Office, national enforcement and US state landscape** — [Cubbbix regulation update](https://cubbbix.com/blog/ai-regulation-november-2026-global-update) (unverified, no primary links)
Reported items include first formal EU information requests to GPAI providers (Oct 14), Italy and Poland national enforcement steps, no comprehensive US federal AI statute, and New York's RAISE Act taking effect Jan 1, 2027.

## 4. Academic articles on AI

**RECLAIM: Can agents reproduce claims of ML papers?** — [arXiv 2609.28850](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28850) (likely September 2026)
A benchmark of 100 NeurIPS 2025 papers; the best agents reproduced roughly 41%, 27% and 15% of results in the run, retrain and reimplement tiers. A good case study on reproducibility.

**On Agentic Manipulation Policies** — [arXiv 2611.09892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09892v1)
A generalist model commanding robot manipulators directly reports 85.6% zero-shot success on a robotics benchmark versus about 43-49% for specialized policies.

**Can Agentic Systems Close the Gap in Radiology with Production AI Tools?** — [arXiv 2611.09836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09836v1)
A simulation showing an agent orchestrating FDA-cleared tools recovers under a third of actionable findings, mostly because no cleared tool covers those classes.

**AI Isn't Getting Dumber, We're Getting Pickier** — [arXiv 2611.14333](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14333v1)
Argues perceived LLM decline reflects rising human standards, using Chatbot Arena data.

**When Should Agents Remember? Falsification-Gated Self-Evolution** — [arXiv 2611.14326](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14326v1)
Agents keep a learned lesson only if it survives verification on new tasks, reducing reliance on outdated strategies.

**Medical Image Segmentation: Foundation Model Adaptation vs Task-Specific Training** — [arXiv 2611.14519](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14519v1)
Task-specific models stay competitive with adapted foundation models, especially with little or shifted data.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: Verify the headline.** Give students three conflicting accounts of the Nvidia CEO story (resigned vs. being ousted vs. investigation). They trace each claim to a primary source, grade source reliability, and write a short note on how LLM-generated or aggregated news can propagate errors. Ties into AI literacy and hallucination discussions.
2. **Lecture and discussion: Can agents reproduce AI research?** Use RECLAIM's run/retrain/reimplement tiers to explain reproducibility, benchmarks and evaluation. Students attempt to reproduce a small scikit-learn result from a paper's description and compare their success rate with the agents'.
3. **Lab: Regulation risk tiers.** Using the EU AI Act Dec 2 prohibitions and marking rules, students classify sample AI applications (deepfake app, resume screener, chatbot) by risk tier and propose a machine-readable marking scheme for generated text, then discuss the California lawsuit's argument for pausing new models.

*Optional Java add-on:* have students compare a thread-pool approach with Structured Concurrency (JEP 543 style) for a parallel model-scoring task.
