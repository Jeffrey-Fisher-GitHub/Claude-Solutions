## 2. AI regulation and policy

**New York signs an AI companion chatbot law for minors**
Source: [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/br-privacy-security-ai-download-november-2026)
The law bars operators from offering "unsafe" companion features to minors, including bots that imply they are human, flatter users, or encourage self-harm or secrecy. The Attorney General can seek up to $25,000 per violation. It takes effect January 1, 2027.

**DOJ launches a Super Intelligence Task Force**
Source: [DOJ press release](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-launches-super-intelligence-task-force)
Announced Nov 5, it will pursue foreign state actors, transnational criminals and corporations that misuse AI. AI Weekly says it absorbs the AI Litigation Task Force created in January 2026.

**White House AI taskforce starts a 120-day clock**
Source: [TLT AI Brief](https://www.tlt.com/insights-and-events/insight/tlts-ai-brief-november-2026) and [a16z Policy Brief](https://a16zpolicy.substack.com/p/icymi-ai-policy-november-update)
A federal coordinating taskforce announced in October has held its first meeting and adopted a charter. It will assess cybersecurity, biosecurity and model-control risks, with a report due in about 120 days. Exact meeting dates were not given in the sources.

**DHS orders DeepSeek products out of federal civilian agencies**
Source: [a16z Policy Brief](https://a16zpolicy.substack.com/p/icymi-ai-policy-november-update)
The order dated Oct 20 bars purchases and requires removal of existing DeepSeek products, including third-party tools built on its models. This is from a single source and I did not check the Federal Register.

**States press OpenAI over child safety**
Source: [a16z Policy Brief](https://a16zpolicy.substack.com/p/icymi-ai-policy-november-update)
Florida's attorney general is seeking a temporary injunction that would restrict OpenAI's new-model development and its service to minors in the state. Eight other states have filed separate suits, and Texas has sued under its deceptive-practices law. Filing dates were not given.

**UK budget funds AI biosecurity and security work**
Source: [TLT AI Brief](https://www.tlt.com/insights-and-events/insight/tlts-ai-brief-november-2026)
The package includes a £165 million biosecurity initiative, £120 million for the AI Security Institute and £60 million for a cyber shield using agentic AI. The sources did not date the budget, so it may fall outside the window.

## 4. Academic articles on AI

These summaries come from bot-generated arXiv digests, not from the papers. Figures are the digests' claims and are unverified.

**First-Token Attraction in Mamba Dynamics**
Source: [arXiv 2611.08303](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08303v1)
Finds that in deep state-space models the first token pulls strongly on later token dynamics. The authors suggest this may inform tokenizer and architecture design.

**SwarmEvolve: Latent Agent Swarms for Evolutionary Discovery**
Source: [arXiv 2611.08483](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08483v1)
Uses swarms of agents working in latent space, rather than long text reasoning, to guide LLM-driven evolutionary search. One digest claims up to a 4x speed-up.

**StrataFlux: Memory-Hierarchy-Native Low-Bit LLM Inference**
Source: [arXiv 2611.08363](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08363v1)
Keeps the most important weight slices at higher precision and puts less important bits in ultra-low-bit caches to limit quantization loss. A digest claims near full-precision accuracy at roughly 3x speed.

**MedGraph: Graph Memory for Long-Term Medical Dialogue**
Source: [arXiv 2611.08433](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08433v1)
Tracks changing patient state across visits with a graph memory to avoid stale or omitted information. A digest reports 27% fewer clinically relevant errors than flat-vector baselines.

**The State of Mechanistic Interpretability for AI Safety**
Source: [arXiv 2611.08438](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08438v1)
A survey of what interpretability research can and cannot verify about network internals, framed as a safety discipline.

## 5. Java development and better code

Only one Java source was readable this run (the JetBrains monthly roundup failed to load), so frameworks, testing and clean-code news are not covered today.

**JDK 27 is generally available as a non-LTS release**
Source: [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4202901/jdk-27-the-new-features-of-java-27.html)
It is a short-term release with six months of Oracle support. The article lists nine features.

**G1 becomes the default garbage collector everywhere (JEP 523)**
Source: [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4202901/jdk-27-the-new-features-of-java-27.html)
Previously G1 was the default only on server-class machines. The goal is that throughput, latency and footprint do not degrade noticeably.

**Compact object headers by default (JEP 534)**
Source: [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4202901/jdk-27-the-new-features-of-java-27.html)
Object headers shrink from 96 to 64 bits on 64-bit platforms, reducing heap size and improving data locality.

**Post-quantum hybrid key exchange for TLS 1.3 (JEP 527)**
Source: [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4202901/jdk-27-the-new-features-of-java-27.html)
Combines a quantum-resistant algorithm with a classical one to protect secure connections against future quantum attacks.

**Language previews continue: primitive patterns (JEP 532), structured concurrency (JEP 533), lazy constants (JEP 531)**
Source: [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4202901/jdk-27-the-new-features-of-java-27.html)
Primitive types can be used in pattern matching, instanceof and switch. Structured concurrency treats related threads as one unit of work, and lazy constants let the JVM optimize deferred initialization like final fields.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: Does the data-center selloff show an AI bubble?** Students compare this week's market headlines with infrastructure announcements (Google's $17B Wyoming campus, OpenAI's below-target raise). In small groups they build a one-page "compute economics" argument, covering cost per token, energy use and revenue, and then critique each other's assumptions. This ties technical concepts to the business and ethics modules.
2. **Lecture demo: Cryptography versus AI agents.** Use the DeepMind RSA result to explain why key length matters, the difference between 1024-bit and 2048-bit RSA, and how AI-assisted search can speed up cryptanalysis. A short Java lab could use `BigInteger` to factor small RSA moduli by brute force and show how the effort grows with key size.
3. **Lab: Evaluating claims in AI news.** Give students three vendor claims from this digest (for example, 81 Firefox bugs found, 35% faster time to first token, near full-precision accuracy at 3x speed). They trace each claim to a primary source, list what is unverified, and design a small benchmark they could run themselves. Extend it with the JDK 27 G1 and compact-headers changes by timing a simple Java workload before and after switching collectors.
