A Secretary's order excludes the vendor's products and services from civilian agency IT. It follows the pattern of restricting adversary-linked AI in government.

A presidential social-media post said large data centers should supply their own electricity and pay communities a dividend. No formal policy action was described, so treat it as a signal only.

**State AI laws reach 280 in 2026** — [TechJack Solutions](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-brief/280-state-ai-laws-one-year-actually-mean-multi-state/) (background)
NCSL counts 280 enacted AI laws through early October, led by deepfake and AI-disclosure measures. Only two comprehensive frameworks passed (Colorado and Connecticut). A federal executive order created a DOJ task force to challenge state laws, but one law-firm analysis says broad preemption lacks a strong legal basis.

**EU AI Act omnibus delays high-risk rules** — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/) (background)
Stand-alone high-risk obligations (hiring, credit, education) move to Dec 2027, and AI embedded in regulated products to Aug 2028. The package adds a ban on systems that generate non-consensual intimate imagery or CSAM and gives the EU AI Office more oversight power. Watermarking grace for existing systems ends Dec 2, 2026.

## 4. Academic articles on AI

Source for this section: [arXiv digest, Oct 26](https://github.com/kakapez/agents-radar/issues/1682). Links are to the arXiv abstracts listed there.

**A Fix for Softmax and Cross-Entropy** — [arXiv 2610.31642](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31642v1)
Points out two numerical issues in common softmax and cross-entropy implementations that hurt gradients at extreme logit values. A good tie-in for how loss functions behave in practice.

**Direct Pass@k Optimization for Thought Pruning** — [arXiv 2610.31667](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31667v1)
Trims redundant reasoning paths at inference time while preserving answer diversity, lowering compute cost for reasoning models.

**Causal Chain of Thought** — [arXiv 2610.31639](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31639v1)
Isolates context between reasoning steps so each step genuinely affects the final answer, addressing "decorative" chains of thought.

**EpiSEM-Bench** — [arXiv 2610.31650](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31650v1)
Tests whether tool-using agents avoid prohibited actions when tool outputs leave out safety-relevant facts.

**Reliable AI-Assisted Decisions via Selective Auditing** — [arXiv 2610.31662](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31662v1)
Shows how auditing only a subset of confidence-scored AI decisions can maintain reliability, relevant to human-in-the-loop design.

**VERA: Responsible-AI evidence dashboard** — [arXiv 2610.31588](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31588v1)
Organizes technical evidence across 18 requirements mapped to EU AI Act compliance for LLM deployments.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: "Why softmax needs care."** Students implement softmax and cross-entropy in Python/NumPy, break it with extreme logits, then apply the log-sum-exp stabilization discussed in the new numerical-bug paper. Extension: compare against a library implementation and plot gradients.
2. **Lecture + role-play: Agents, guardrails, and auditing.** Use the EpiSEM-Bench, selective-auditing, Qwen3.8 egress, and Cloudflare leak stories. Teams design a sandbox and audit policy for a tool-using agent (egress controls, per-tenant isolation tests, sampled human review) and defend it against the others' "red team."
3. **Case study: Reading an AI IPO and the compute economy.** Students read the Anthropic filing coverage and the chip-lease financing, then build a simple spreadsheet or Java model comparing revenue, compute cost, and financing structures under different growth assumptions. Pair with a short discussion of the EU and U.S. policy items on what regulation does to deployment timelines (informational only, not investment advice).
