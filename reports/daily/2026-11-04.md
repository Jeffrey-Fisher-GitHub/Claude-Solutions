## 2. AI regulation and policy

**US states keep moving** - [Cubbbix](https://cubbbix.com/blog/ai-regulation-november-2026-global-update), [a16z](https://a16zpolicy.substack.com/p/icymi-ai-policy-november-update)

**EU: Digital Omnibus delayed high-risk rules, but Dec 2 bans loom** - [Cubbbix](https://cubbbix.com/blog/ai-regulation-november-2026-global-update), [FutureAGI timeline](https://futureagi.com/blog/eu-ai-act-timeline-digital-omnibus-2027/)
High-risk obligations were pushed to Dec 2027 (Annex III) and Aug 2028 (Annex I). Transparency rules and AI Office enforcement powers have applied since Aug 2, 2026. On Dec 2, 2026, bans on AI that generates non-consensual intimate imagery or CSAM start, along with the end of the grace period for machine-readable marking of generated content. Preliminary guidance on Oct 28 said AI agents generally count as AI systems.

**Elsewhere** - [Cubbbix](https://cubbbix.com/blog/ai-regulation-november-2026-global-update)
China released draft AI export controls (consultation to Nov 15), Italy and Poland activated EU AI Act enforcement pieces in October, and Singapore's MAS issued AI risk-management guidelines on Oct 7. The UK still has no AI bill before Parliament.

## 4. Academic articles on AI

**Weight-space programming: AI editing other networks** - [Blog write-up](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/) (via The Neuron)
Reportedly, MIT researchers showed a model can inspect and modify another model's weights. Changing 0.016% of one Qwen model's weights raised its MATH-500 score from 7.9% to 48.1%. This is a blog summary, not a peer-reviewed paper, so treat the figures cautiously.

**TokenEconomizer: cost control for coding agents** - [arXiv 2610.22732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22732)
Cuts recurring token spend for coding agents by pruning irrelevant tools, adding short skill hints, and halting repeated failure loops.

**Harness^2: recursive agent harnessing** - [arXiv 2610.14224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14224)
Improves how agent scaffolding runs by refining at the task level and learning at the harness level, without needing ground-truth verifiers.

**FreeToken: edge serving of Mixture-of-Experts models** - [arXiv 2608.16157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16157)
Spreads computation across mixed local hardware so large open-weight MoE models can run on personal machines.

**Tug of Watts: energy vs. accuracy of LLMs on medical QA** - [arXiv 2611.02889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02889v1)
Examines how much energy LLMs use relative to their accuracy on medical question answering, a good fit for sustainability discussions.

**Uni-robot-agent: open models for robot-use agents** - [arXiv 2611.01560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01560)
Reports open-weight robot agents beating proprietary ones on zero-shot manipulation, and suggests training four core capabilities together.

## 5. Java development and better code

**Oracle October CPU: patch now** - [Java Bulletin Digest #110](https://javabulletin.substack.com/p/digest-110-patch-your-jdk-this-week)
Oracle's October update fixes 55 Java SE flaws, 49 remotely exploitable without login. Fixed builds are 27.0.1, 25.0.5, 21.0.13, 17.0.21, 11.0.33 and 8u511. A separate alert (CVE-2026-91403, CVSS 9.1) distrusts compromised eMudhra intermediate CAs.

**JDK 27 (GA Sept 15): what changed** - [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4202901/jdk-27-the-new-features-of-java-27.html)
Nine JEPs, including G1 as the default collector everywhere, post-quantum hybrid TLS 1.3 key exchange, compact object headers by default, and JFR redaction of sensitive data. Structured concurrency is in its seventh preview, and the newsletter says it goes final in JDK 28 (due March 9, 2027).

**Spring Boot 4.2 and Spring Framework 7.1 release candidates** - [Java Bulletin](https://javabulletin.substack.com/p/digest-110-patch-your-jdk-this-week)
RC1s landed Oct 22 with GA expected in November. RestTemplate is deprecated for removal in favor of RestClient.

**Structured concurrency tip: race two calls** - [Java Bulletin](https://javabulletin.substack.com/p/digest-110-patch-your-jdk-this-week)
`StructuredTaskScope` with `Joiner.anySuccessfulResultOrThrow()` returns the first success and cancels the loser, a clean pattern for primary-vs-replica calls.

**Faster builds and a classic concurrency trap** - [Java Bulletin](https://javabulletin.substack.com/p/digest-110-patch-your-jdk-this-week)
A Deutsche Bank team halved PR build times (about 18 to 9 minutes) with Maven's build cache. The weekly interview question is a reminder that a singleton with a plain `HashMap` cache is still unsafe under concurrent threads.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: "Is it really close to RSI?" evidence-reading exercise.** Give students Anthropic's reported RSI warning and the weight-space-programming blog post. They identify the claim, the evidence, and what a benchmark like MATH-500 does and does not show. Then they write a one-page assessment separating measured results from forecasts.
2. **Lecture + demo: AI agents, permissions, and regulation.** Use Claude Duet, GPT-6 parallel agents, Apple's filing warning, and the EU's "agents are AI systems" guidance to discuss what an agent is, why approval gates exist, and how law maps to design. Students draft a permission-and-approval policy for a campus scheduling agent.
3. **Hands-on lab: cost and energy of inference.** Using the TokenEconomizer and "Tug of Watts" papers, students run a small open model on a fixed question set, log tokens, latency and accuracy, and compare a baseline prompt against a pruned prompt. They report an accuracy-vs-cost tradeoff. Optional Java extension: use JDK 27 structured concurrency to race two model endpoints and cancel the slower one.
