## 2. AI regulation and policy

**SAFE AI Act outline (US House, bipartisan)**
Source: [Rep. Jacobs press release](https://sarajacobs.house.gov/news/press-releases/jacobs-maloy-beyer-release-outline-for-sweeping-bipartisan-ai-safety-legislation); [outline PDF](https://beyer.house.gov/uploadedfiles/safe_ai_act_outline.pdf)
Reps. Jacobs, Maloy and Beyer released an outline for federal frontier-AI rules: required safety frameworks, incident reporting, greater transparency, and stronger enforcement. It is an outline only, not yet an introduced bill.

**AUDIT AI Act (US Senate)**
Source: [Sen. Warner bill text](https://www.warner.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/EHF26804.pdf)
Senators Warner, Kennedy, Alsobrooks and Rounds proposed that public companies disclose whether they have AI governance policies and a board-level AI committee. Companies with major AI exposure would standardize disclosure of risks such as third-party model reliance, compute concentration and AI-related off-balance-sheet debt.

**Commerce interim rule on advanced computing**
Source: [Politico Pro rule mirror](https://regulations.politicopro.com/rule/509e87be-9c42-ec94-629d-6d63ed792a2a)
The Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule aimed at stopping foreign actors from using US-technology-based advanced computing in ways that threaten national security. The link is a mirror, not the Federal Register, so check the primary text before citing.

**Open comment periods**
Sources: [ACUS on generative AI in agency adjudication](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23539/policies-on-the-use-of-generative-super-intelligence-in-agency-adjudication-request-for-comments) (comments close Jan 19, 2027); [FCC IoT cybersecurity labeling](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/23/2026-23812/cybersecurity-labeling-for-internet-of-things) (comments due Dec 23, 2026).
Both are good real-world examples for students of how AI rules get shaped by public input.

**EU: ban on nudify and CSAM-generating systems starts Dec 2**
Source: [wpnews](https://wpnews.pro/news/eu-ai-nudify-app-ban-puts-image-model-safeguards-to-the-test-on-december-2) (original: [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/eu-ai-nudify-app-ban-puts-image-model-safeguards-to-the-test-on-december-2/))
Per this secondary report, the AI Act prohibition applies from Dec 2, 2026, with no transition period for systems already on the market, and fines up to EUR 35 million or 7% of turnover. Machine-readable marking duties for older generative systems apply the same day. I did not confirm the date on the Commission's own page.

**US states:** The same article reports that Oregon and Virginia sued SpaceX over its Grok chatbot under consumer-protection laws on Nov 23. The allegations are unproven and I found no primary link.

## 4. Academic AI articles (arXiv, cs.AI listing of Nov 26)

**Context Scaling: A New Frontier of AI Scaling**: [arXiv 2611.33160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33160)
A position paper arguing that many deployed failures come from missing task context. It proposes context as a third scaling budget beside training and inference compute.

**Constraint-Aware Test-Time Scoring for LLM and VLM Candidate Selection**: [arXiv 2611.33161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33161)
Selecting the best of several model answers works best with a few hard, grounded checks plus comparison across candidates. Soft judge scores add little.

**What, Who, and When: Structured Harm Assessment for Mental Health Safety**: [arXiv 2611.33263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33263)
Proposes a clinician-grounded harm taxonomy and a 35B guard model reported to match larger models at about 10x lower latency.

**The Platonic Representation Hypothesis in Jacobian-Space**: [arXiv 2611.33304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33304)
Reports that language and vision models' internal behavior can be approximately aligned by a single rotation, which transfers to words not used in fitting.

Note: these come from the start of a very long listing and I only read the abstracts' summaries on the listing page.

## 5. Java and better code

**JDK 27 is generally available (Sep 15, 2026; non-LTS)**
Sources: [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4202901/jdk-27-the-new-features-of-java-27.html); [Azul](https://www.azul.com/blog/jdk-27-the-quiet-before-the-storm/)
Nine JEPs. Final features include G1 as the default collector on all platforms (JEP 523), post-quantum hybrid key exchange for TLS 1.3 (JEP 527), compact object headers on by default (JEP 534, cited as 22% less heap and 8% less CPU on SPECjbb2015), and JFR redaction of sensitive arguments (JEP 536). Previews and incubators include lazy constants (531), primitive patterns (532), structured concurrency (533), the Vector API (537) and PEM encodings (538). The two articles disagree on some preview counts, so check the JEP pages before quoting them.

**Patching strategy**
Source: [Azul](https://www.azul.com/blog/jdk-27-the-quiet-before-the-storm/)
Azul says the first JDK Critical Security Patch Update delayed JDK 27's release candidate by two weeks and links this to AI models getting better at finding vulnerabilities. It also advertises a critical memory-corruption issue; that is a vendor claim, so verify it against the CVE record.

**Looking ahead to JDK 28**
Azul reports a standard JSON API and the first Project Valhalla features (JEP 401 value objects, JEP 539) as candidates. Neither article covered testing tools or clean-code topics, so I have nothing reliable to report there today.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: speculative decoding by hand.** Using Tencent's DFly2 as the hook, students simulate a small "draft" model and a larger "verifier" over a toy vocabulary in Python. They count accepted vs. rejected draft tokens and measure the speed-up at different acceptance rates. This reinforces next-token prediction, probability, and why vendor throughput numbers need methodology.
2. **Lecture and debate: compute as policy.** Use the chip-curb stories and Canada's 80% compute subsidy. Groups argue for or against compute export limits and public compute funding, then identify what evidence (release cadence, cost data) would settle it. Pair with the NIST AI RMF material already in the course.
3. **Lab: "best-of-N with checks."** Based on the constraint-aware scoring paper, students generate several answers to a coding or math question, then pick a winner by (a) a model-judge score and (b) hard checks such as unit tests. They compare which method picks correct answers more often, and discuss the Java 27 angle by writing the checks as JUnit tests.
