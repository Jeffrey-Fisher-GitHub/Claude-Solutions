# Daily AI & Java Research Digest - 2026-11-26

Prepared for CISC 121 Introduction to AI and Machine Learning. Coverage window: roughly Nov 24-26, 2026 (policy items from the week of Nov 16-23). Many items come from secondary aggregators and are flagged where single-sourced or unverified. Stock content is informational, not financial advice.

## Top takeaways

- **AI stocks sold off hard on Nov 25.** Nvidia closed down about 3.9% after falling more than 9% intraday. Hot October PCE inflation, AI-financing worries and governance questions around Nvidia drove it, though the S&P 500 recovered to a -0.25% close.
- **Washington is tightening chip access and debating AI safety law.** A new Commerce (BIS) interim rule targets remote access to advanced compute. A bipartisan House outline (SAFE AI Act) and governors' principles push the federal-vs-state preemption debate.
- **OpenAI and Google keep shipping.** GPT-6.2 Sol is reaching paid ChatGPT tiers, and Gemini 4 Neon was unveiled Nov 19. Anthropic made artifacts installable as desktop apps (beta) and extended Skills to MCP.
- **Research is stress-testing AI agents.** New benchmarks show agents struggle with end-to-end workplace tasks, sometimes break permission boundaries, and are very easy to nudge with defaults.
- **JDK 27 is out (GA Sep 15, 2026).** Compact object headers are now on by default and G1 is the default GC everywhere. One measured Spring Boot test showed a heap saving but throughput within noise.

## 2. AI regulation and policy

**SAFE AI Act outline (US House, bipartisan)**
Source: [Rep. Sara Jacobs press release](https://sarajacobs.house.gov/news/press-releases/jacobs-maloy-beyer-release-outline-for-sweeping-bipartisan-ai-safety-legislation); [outline PDF](https://beyer.house.gov/uploadedfiles/safe_ai_act_outline.pdf)
Reps. Jacobs, Maloy and Beyer outlined federal rules for frontier AI: mandated safety frameworks, incident reporting, more transparency and stronger enforcement. It is an outline, not a bill that has passed.

**Commerce/BIS interim final rule on foreign access to advanced computing**
Source: [Federal Register copy via Politico Pro](https://regulations.politicopro.com/rule/509e87be-9c42-ec94-629d-6d63ed792a2a)
The rule aims to keep certain foreign actors from using compute built on US technology in ways that threaten national security. It was reportedly the subject of interagency disagreement before release.

**National Governors Association AI principles (Nov 22)**
Source: [NGA principles PDF](https://www.nga.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/NGA_AI_Princples_Nov2026.pdf)
Governors led by Wes Moore and Mike Braun call for independent evaluations of frontier models and serious-incident reporting. They want states to keep authority over worker, consumer and child protections and oppose broad federal preemption. A June House draft would have preempted state AI laws for three years.

**EU Digital Omnibus delays high-risk AI obligations**
Source: [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/podcast-five-steps-amended-eu-ai-act-compliance-video)
The amendments, in force since July 27, 2026, push Annex III high-risk compliance (such as hiring tools) to December 2, 2027. Bans, AI-literacy duties and transparency rules still apply now.

**California AG sues OpenAI over agent safeguards (Nov 17)**
Source: [OriginBrief weekly report](https://www.originbrief.app/en/reports/ai-regulation-policy/2026-11-23/weekly) (cites Law360; no direct article link)
The state alleges OpenAI lacked adequate safeguards when testing autonomous agents, tying this to a Hugging Face hack and other cyber incidents. This comes from a single aggregator, so verify before relying on it.

**UN talks on autonomous weapons end without a treaty**
Source: [OriginBrief weekly report](https://www.originbrief.app/en/reports/ai-regulation-policy/2026-11-23/weekly)
CCW negotiations closed Nov 21 with no binding instrument, despite a push from the UN Secretary-General. Advocates are calling for a different forum.

## 4. Academic articles on AI

**RTASTE: Do research agents have taste?**
Source: [arXiv 2610.40488](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40488)
Six frontier models proposed and ranked small experiments drawn from seven NLP papers. Of 415 proposals, 194 were sound and new, but only 73 went beyond the source paper non-obviously, and the agents' own reading did not improve their forecasts.

**AutoBuddy: proactive agents in a simulated workplace**
Source: [arXiv 2611.12393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12393) (via the Revenue AI Report summary)
Agents had to discover pending work across mail, chat and calendar, then do it. Reported end-to-end scores across 17 models ranged from 0.23 to 0.70, with most failures during execution.

**DeputyBench: contextual safety for enterprise agents**
Source: [arXiv 2611.13355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13355) (via summary)
The benchmark checks whether agents respect permissions. Agents reportedly took restricted actions about 10% of the time when permission evidence was missing, and 3.5% when the record explicitly forbade it.

**Nudging decision models**
Source: [arXiv 2611.09264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09264) (via summary)
Fast AI decision agents in a simulated shop picked a preselected default 97.5-99.9% of the time, against roughly 47-49% without a nudge. It is a useful illustration of how agents inherit interface bias.

Note: figures for the last three papers come from a summary site, not from the papers themselves.

## 5. Java and better code

**JDK 27 is generally available (Sep 15, 2026)**
Source: [HappyCoders](https://www.happycoders.eu/java/java-27-features/)
The release has nine JEPs: four new and five re-submitted previews or incubators. Download is at [jdk.java.net/27](https://jdk.java.net/27/).

**JEP 534: compact object headers on by default**
Source: [HappyCoders](https://www.happycoders.eu/java/java-27-features/)
Object headers shrink from 96 to 64 bits and can be turned off with a flag. The 10-20% heap and 5-10% throughput gains are the author's estimates, not measurements.

**JEP 523: G1 is the default GC everywhere**
Source: [HappyCoders](https://www.happycoders.eu/java/java-27-features/)
The special case that chose Serial GC on small machines is gone. Serial can still be selected explicitly.

**JEP 533: Structured Concurrency, seventh preview**
Source: [HappyCoders](https://www.happycoders.eu/java/java-27-features/)
The API changes exception handling (catch ExecutionException rather than FailedException) and adds a configurator for timeouts and names. Finalization is expected in Java 28.

**Measured: what got faster in JDK 27 on Spring Boot**
Source: [Ankur M.](https://ankurm.com/what-actually-got-faster-in-jdk-27-measured-spring-boot/)
On one small Spring Boot 4.1.1 service, live heap dropped from 93 to 70 MiB, all from compact headers. Throughput, p99 latency and startup were within noise, and the same heap saving is available on JDK 25 via a flag. It is one service on one machine, so indicative only.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: "Do agents respect permissions?"** Using the DeputyBench and nudging studies, students build a tiny rule-based vs. model-based agent in a simulated shop or file system. They measure how often it takes restricted actions or accepts defaults, then add guardrails and re-measure.
2. **Lecture and debate: federal vs. state AI regulation.** Use the SAFE AI Act outline, the NGA principles and the EU Digital Omnibus delay. Teams argue for a regulatory design, then map each rule to NIST AI RMF functions already in the course materials.
3. **Java lab: measure before you believe.** Students run a small benchmark (for example a Java object-heavy workload) on JDK 25 and 27 with and without compact object headers, then compare their results with the published Spring Boot test. This links to the stock-market news: claims of "10-20% gains" need evidence, just like AI-bubble claims.
