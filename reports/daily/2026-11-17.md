## 2. AI regulation and policy

**EU proposes the Digital Fairness Act (Nov 11)** - Source: [CASRAI summary](https://casrai.org/news/eu-digital-fairness-act-ai-consumer-proposal-2026)
The Commission's proposal would require adjustable approval settings and disclosure of paid recommendations for AI purchasing agents, and controls to disable addictive design, including automated prompts that simulate relationships. It also limits profiling-based ads and personalised pricing aimed at vulnerable consumers. It is only a proposal; Parliament and Council must still negotiate it.

## 4. Academic AI articles

**Large Language Models without QK-Normalization** - Source: [arXiv 2611.10560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10560) (Nov 7)
The authors argue QK-Norm, a common stabilizer in attention layers, discards useful token-magnitude information. Their QK-MAP method encodes magnitude as bounded phase patterns and reports lower training and validation loss than standard attention and QK-Norm from 190M to 7B parameters.

**Scientific Coding with LLMs: Evidence from Finite Element Analysis** - Source: [arXiv 2611.21176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21176) (Nov 16)
Extending the FEM-Bench benchmark to 17 tasks and 15 models, the paper finds the best model passes 87.1% of single-pass trials. Agentic setups reach 96.5% Pass@3 but only 77.1% Pass^3, a gap the authors attribute largely to weak self-verification and thin test coverage.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: Why agents need verification.** Using the FEM-Bench result (Pass@3 vs Pass^3), students write a small Java or Python harness that runs an AI-generated function against a test suite three times and computes both metrics, then discuss why consistency matters more than best-of-n in real work.
2. **Lecture and debate: Regulating AI products.** Use the EU Dec 2 marking requirement, the Digital Fairness Act proposal on shopping agents, and US state-versus-federal tension. Teams map one AI product to the rules that would apply and argue what a compliance checklist should include.
3. **Data-analysis lab: AI hype versus market data.** Students pull a week of price data for Nvidia, AMD and the Nasdaq (CSV provided), compute returns and correlations, and then discuss how news events (CEO exit, chip curbs, yields) relate to moves, with an emphasis on correlation not being causation. Bonus: add a Java stream-based calculation and a unit test, tying in the clean-code and testing news.
