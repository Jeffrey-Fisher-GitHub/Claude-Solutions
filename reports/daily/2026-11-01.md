## 2. AI regulation and policy

**EU Digital Omnibus and AI Act implementation**
Source: [CDT Europe AI Bulletin, October 2026](https://cdt.org/insights/cdt-europes-ai-bulletin-october-2026)
The Council's Oct 21 position keeps "legitimate interest" as a legal basis for AI data processing and would treat pseudonymised data as outside GDPR in some cases. The Commission published the first harmonised AI Act standard (quality management) and postponed high-risk obligations to at least December 2027. A consultation on enforcement against general-purpose AI providers closes Nov 9.

**European Parliament pushes for binding global AI rules**
Source: [CDT Europe AI Bulletin](https://cdt.org/insights/cdt-europes-ai-bulletin-october-2026)
MEPs on Oct 22 rejected self-regulation and discussed a binding international AI treaty. Several called for new liability rules after the AI Liability Directive was withdrawn.

**US federal and state landscape**
Source: [Wilson Sonsini tracker](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/recent-ai-regulatory-developments-in-the-united-states.html)
The administration's framework asks Congress to preempt burdensome state AI laws, while California's frontier transparency (SB 53) and automated-decision rules are already phasing in. New York's RAISE Act was amended to align with California, with enforcement delayed to January 2027. Colorado's AI Act remains uncertain amid litigation.

**School AI standard, UK probe and data-center fights**
Source: [AI Weekly, Nov 1](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today)
A Microsoft–teachers' union school AI standard took effect Nov 1, banning use of student data for model training. The UK CMA opened an inquiry into Meta's terms for using publisher content in AI features. A federal judge let a Clean Air Act suit over gas turbines at xAI's Memphis data center proceed.

**Mistral's open-weight release and cyber-misuse debate**
Source: [AI Weekly, Nov 1](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today)
Anthropic's usage policy update (effective Nov 12) bans unauthorized distillation of outputs. This sits alongside open-weight releases that raise policy questions about control.

## 4. Academic articles

**Language Models Learn to Improve Language Models (LM2LM)**
Source: [arXiv 2610.23344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23344)
The authors train a model with reinforcement learning to propose improvements to other models, using a frozen LLM as a cheap "research world model" for rewards and periodically grounding it with real experiments. A 27B model trained this way reportedly beat a larger frontier system on held-out tasks at about a third less data cost.

**LLMs Extend Known Research But Do Not Create New Directions**
Source: [arXiv 2610.17439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17439)
Comparing 524k LLM hypotheses against 26.9M papers, the study finds LLM ideas land farther from existing work but have 15-23% lower "orthogonal novelty." The authors argue that opening new research directions remains human-led.

**Language Models as AI Research World Models**
Source: [arXiv 2610.12235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12235)
The companion paper behind the LM2LM approach, on using language models to predict the outcomes of AI research experiments. (Abstract not reviewed in detail.)

**Agentic AI across digital, social, virtual and physical environments**
Source: [arXiv 2609.04894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04894)
A survey of progress and limits of agentic systems. (Listed from search results; not reviewed in detail.)

## 5. Java development and better code

**JDK 27 released**
Source: [Azul, JDK 27 overview](https://www.azul.com/blog/jdk-27-the-quiet-before-the-storm/)
Nine JEPs, four final. Compact object headers are on by default (JEP cites ~22% less heap and ~8% less CPU on a benchmark), and G1 is now the default collector everywhere. Also added: post-quantum hybrid key exchange for TLS 1.3 and in-process redaction of secrets in Flight Recorder data.

**Preview features continue**
Source: [Azul](https://www.azul.com/blog/jdk-27-the-quiet-before-the-storm/)
Structured concurrency (seventh preview), lazy constants, primitive types in patterns, and the Vector API incubator continue with small changes. Good material for discussing safer concurrency and pattern matching.

**JDK 28 preview**
Source: [Azul](https://www.azul.com/blog/jdk-27-the-quiet-before-the-storm/)
Early targets include a standard JSON API, a deprecation of the macOS/x64 port, and the first Project Valhalla pieces (value objects, strict field initialization).

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: "Can the monitor be fooled?"** Use the Anthropic monitor-evasion result and the DeepSeek shutdown test to have students build a toy classifier-based "monitor" on text data, then try to craft inputs that evade it. Discuss why single-source, small-sample safety claims need replication.
2. **Lecture + debate: open weights vs. gated access.** Compare Mistral Large 4 (downloadable), GPT-6 Rosalind (trusted access) and the cancelled GPT-6.1 Astra release. Students argue policy positions using the EU Digital Omnibus and US preemption developments.
3. **Mini-lab: novelty and embeddings.** Based on the "LLMs don't create new directions" paper, students embed a small set of abstracts, then measure distance versus direction for LLM-generated ideas, and tie in a short Java snippet (JDK 27 pattern matching) to compute cosine similarity.
