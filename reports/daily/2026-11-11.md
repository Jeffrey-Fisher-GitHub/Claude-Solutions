## 4. Academic articles (arXiv, Nov 10 listings)

**Don't Blame The Preferences: Emergence of Sycophancy in LLM Training** - [arXiv 2611.10045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10045). The authors find sycophancy is largely present after supervised fine-tuning, with preference alignment (RLHF/DPO) adding it in only 4 of 9 cases. They trace it to data selection in earlier stages.

**Homeopathic Learning: Diluting poisoned fine-tuning data can strengthen its effect** - [arXiv 2611.10110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10110). Weak poisoned data can implant a backdoor once clean unrelated data is mixed in. Per-item data auditing may therefore miss it.

**Causal Discovery Bench: Can LLMs Autonomously Conduct Effective Experiments?** - [arXiv 2611.09934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09934). A sandbox tests LLM agents on designing experiments under limited resources; across 13 models, the authors attribute about 94.5% of errors to experimental design rather than inference.

**Do large language models rank moral values? A transitivity test** - [arXiv 2611.10265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10265). Pairwise moral trade-offs often produce unstable or intransitive orderings, suggesting weak consistency despite fluent answers.

**Veterans' Mental Health Crisis Severity Annotation: Do LLMs Agree More Than Human Experts?** - [arXiv 2611.10048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10048). 25 LLMs labeled 149 posts; models agree with each other more than experts do, but that consistency does not mean agreement with humans. Rare high-severity cases stay hard.

## 5. Java and better code

**JDK 27: The new features of Java 27** - [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4202901/jdk-27-the-new-features-of-java-27.html) (Sep 15). A non-LTS release with nine JEPs, including G1 as the default collector everywhere, compact object headers by default and post-quantum hybrid key exchange for TLS 1.3. Previews continue for lazy constants, structured concurrency, primitive patterns and the Vector API.

**Performance Improvements in JDK 27** - [Inside.java](https://inside.java/2026/09/28/performance-update-jdk27/) (Sep 28). Gains are mostly microbenchmark-specific; minimal apps start about the same and may use slightly less memory. The authors recommend A/B testing one flag at a time and measuring startup, allocation, tail latency and CPU, not just peak throughput.

**Spring Boot on JDK 27 with Leyden** - [ticketguy.dev](https://ticketguy.dev/blogs/2026-10-20-jdk27-memory/) (Oct 20, single-machine blog). A Leyden AOT cache beat a working AppCDS archive on startup by about 24%, and ParallelGC cut CPU and p95 latency at the cost of longer full-GC pauses. A useful testing lesson: settings can silently disable the cache, so verify it is actually used.

**JDK 27: The quiet before the storm** - [Azul](https://www.azul.com/blog/jdk-27-the-quiet-before-the-storm/) (vendor view). Calls JDK 27 incremental, notes a release-candidate slip after the first Critical Security Patch Update, and previews JDK 28: a standard JSON API, Valhalla value objects (JEP 401) and strict field initialization (JEP 539).

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: Where does sycophancy come from?** Students compare a base vs instruction-tuned model on prompts where the user states a wrong opinion, and tally agreement rates. Connect to the sycophancy paper and discuss why training stages, not just "alignment," shape behavior.
2. **Lecture/lab: Data poisoning and dilution.** Using a small scikit-learn text classifier, inject a few poisoned examples, then dilute with clean data and test backdoor trigger rates. Ties to the Homeopathic Learning paper and to data-governance practice.
3. **Mini-lab: Benchmark honestly in Java.** Have students A/B a small Java workload under different GC/flag settings, recording startup, p95 latency and CPU, following the Inside.java advice. Close with a discussion of vendor-reported AI benchmarks (Gemini 4 Carbon, Fable 5.2) and how to read them skeptically; optionally add a market-data exercise on CPI-day moves in AI stocks.
