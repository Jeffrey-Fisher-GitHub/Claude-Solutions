## 1. AI industry news

**Anthropic pitches a federal AI safety bill** — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/anthropic-pitches-ai-safety-bill-that-may-block-a-model-if-50-people-could-be-killed)
A draft circulated to Congress would let a model release be blocked if it posed an imminent risk of mass casualties or over $1B in damage (bioweapon, loss of control, cyberattack). Independent evaluators registered with Commerce would have 72 hours to report, and Commerce would then ask a federal judge to halt release. Critics question how evaluators paid by labs can remain independent.

**DeepSeek open-sources DeepInfer** — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/deepseek-open-sources-deepinfer-its-v41-flash-serving-stack)
DeepSeek published the production inference stack behind its V4.1-Flash model under Apache 2.0, supporting both Nvidia and Huawei accelerators. This is a useful example of how serving infrastructure, not just model weights, is becoming open.

**Stanford's Halcyon T2I image models** — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/stanfords-halcyon-t2i-family-beats-internet-pretrained-peers-at-275x-less)
Stanford and Radical Numerics report text-to-image models trained without language pretraining that beat larger internet-pretrained peers using far less compute. If it holds up, it challenges the "scale everything" assumption.

**MemT-1.0 memory-native LLMs** — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/memt-10-memory-native-llms-cut-kv-storage-66x-at-128k-tokens)
A paper claims building memory into the model can shrink the storage for a 128K-token history by roughly 66x versus a full KV cache. Relevant to the cost and latency of long-context applications.

**Otter.ai raises $320M non-dilutive capital** — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/otterai-raises-320m-in-non-dilutive-capital-from-general-catalysts-customer)
The meeting-transcription company reports passing $100M in annual recurring revenue and 40M users, financed through revenue-based capital rather than equity. A concrete case of an AI application business reaching scale.

## 2. AI regulation and policy
*(Mostly from a weekly report dated Oct 19; details are second-hand.)*

**Eight states sue OpenAI** — [Tech Policy Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/another-week-of-lawsuits-piling-up-at-openai)
On Oct 13–14, attorneys general from Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska and West Virginia filed suits alleging misleading safety claims, misuse of children's data and addictive design. Some seek injunctions that could pause model development, and five private suits were filed alongside.

**EU AI Act enforcement begins to bite** — [Tech Policy Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/is-europe-ready-for-advanced-ai-the-growing-debate-over-liability)
Since enforcement powers activated on Aug 2, the AI Office has sent information requests to OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta about general-purpose AI obligations. Meanwhile MEPs are pressing for new liability legislation after the AI Liability Directive was withdrawn. The first harmonised standard, EN 18286, has also been published ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/eu-publishes-en-18286-first-ai-act-harmonised-standard)).

**US federal: Executive Order 14434 and NIST changes** — [NIST CAISSI](https://www.nist.gov/caissi)
The Sept 29 order reportedly renames NIST's AI standards center to emphasize "super intelligence" and to resist foreign rules seen as burdening US firms. Treat details as unverified until checked against the order text.

**Treasury's first outbound-investment penalty** — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/united-states-outbound-hole-your-compliance-manual-can-be-costly)
On Oct 7 Treasury fined a venture firm $200,000 for failing to notify it of a small investment by its Chinese fund subsidiary in an AI/robotics company. It signals that AI-related investment screening is now actively enforced.

**Singapore and UK** — [IMDA](https://www.imda.gov.sg/resources/press-releases-factsheets-and-speeches/new-initiatives-to-strengthen-ai-safety-and-resilience), [Tech Policy Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/uk-election-watchdog-develops-ai-system-to-flag-deepfakes-within-3-hours)
Singapore announced layered AI safety and resilience initiatives (Oct 14), saying no single safeguard suffices. The UK Electoral Commission is building a tool to alert candidates to major deepfakes within about three hours.

## 4. Academic articles (trending on Hugging Face Papers)
Source: [HF Trending Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/trending)

**Harness^2: Recursive Agent Harnessing for an Open World** — [arXiv 2610.14224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14224)
Tunes the scaffolding around an agent at both task level and over time, improving benchmark scores without ground-truth verifiers. Good fodder for discussing why the "wrapper" matters as much as the model.

**MOSS-Image Technical Report** — [arXiv 2610.23374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23374)
An open diffusion-transformer image model that adds prompt enhancement and reports leading open-weight results on long-text and layout benchmarks.

**The Other Half of the Memory Wall: Serving 35B MoEs from SSD** — [arXiv 2609.18063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18063)
Predicts which experts a mixture-of-experts model will need so large models can run on consumer hardware with little active memory, using adapters to recover quality.

**Finance-model papers: Kronos and TradingAgents** — [Kronos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02739), [TradingAgents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138)
Two older papers trending again: a foundation model for candlestick data and a multi-agent LLM system that mimics a trading firm. Useful for discussing overfitting and backtest caveats in simulated results.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: "Same model, different harness."** Using the Harness^2 paper as motivation, students run one small open-weight model on a fixed task set with three scaffolds (plain prompt, chain-of-thought, tool-use loop) and compare accuracy, cost and failure modes.
2. **Lecture + debate: Who decides if a model ships?** Use Anthropic's draft bill, the EU AI Office requests and the state suits against OpenAI. Students role-play lab, evaluator, regulator and judge, then critique the independence problem of lab-paid evaluators.
3. **Lab: Benchmarking honestly in Java.** Students benchmark a simple ML inference routine (for example k-NN or logistic regression in Java) with JMH, compare to a naive timing loop, and explain why same-JVM load generation and uncontrolled warmup produce misleading numbers, tying in the JDK 27 performance posts and the simulated-backtest caveats from the trading papers.
