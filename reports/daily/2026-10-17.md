## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: Build a simple model router.** Give students three mock models with different cost/accuracy profiles and a set of tasks of varying difficulty. They implement a rule-based or small-classifier router, then compare cost and accuracy against always using the best model, echoing the Vals AI "perfect hindsight" gap.
2. **Lecture and debate: Who is liable when agents misbehave?** Use the EU AI Office cases, the Cantwell/Rounds letter and the liability-versus-rules argument. Students read two short excerpts and argue for a rules-based or liability-based approach, linking to the NIST AI RMF in the project files.
3. **Hands-on: Multi-agent coordination and bubble-or-not analysis.** Part A: a small grid-world where agents share memory and learn by trial and error, inspired by the Minecraft result. Part B: using Oct 16 price moves and the Anthropic valuation story, discuss growth rates versus valuations and how AI spending is financed (debt risk), with a Java or Python script that computes returns and drawdowns from a CSV.
