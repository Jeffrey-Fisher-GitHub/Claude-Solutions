# Daily AI & Java Research Digest — 2026-11-02

*Prepared for CISC 121 Introduction to AI and Machine Learning. Coverage window: roughly Oct 29 – Nov 2, 2026. Summaries are paraphrased; several benchmark figures are lab self-reports. Stock content is informational only, not financial advice.*

## Top takeaways

- **Open-weight frontier models keep arriving.** Mistral released the weights of its roughly 1-trillion-parameter Large 4 (Oct 29), joining Meta's Muse Spark Open and DeepSeek/Moonshot updates, so the open–closed gap is narrowing.
- **AI capex is now the market's main question.** Alphabet fell about 10% premarket on a revenue miss and negative free cash flow, while Microsoft rose on 48% Azure growth; Anthropic's public S-1 (ticker ANT) added to IPO buzz.
- **EU keeps building enforcement machinery while delaying deadlines.** First harmonised AI Act standard is cited, a GPAI enforcement consultation closes Nov 9, and high-risk obligations slip to at least Dec 2027.
- **AI-tool supply-chain risk is real.** A trojanized Claude Code VS Code extension sat in Cursor's marketplace for about 11 hours.
- **Java 27 is out and JDK 28 is taking shape**, with G1 default everywhere, compact object headers by default, post-quantum TLS, and Valhalla value classes reportedly headed for preview in JDK 28.

## 2. AI Regulation & Policy

**EU Digital Omnibus: Council position (Oct 21)** — [CDT Europe bulletin](https://cdt.org/insights/cdt-europes-ai-bulletin-october-2026)
The Council would treat legitimate interest as a usable legal basis for AI-related personal-data processing, with safeguards, and let pseudonymised data fall outside GDPR scope in some cases.

**First harmonised AI Act standard cited** — [CDT Europe bulletin](https://cdt.org/insights/cdt-europes-ai-bulletin-october-2026), [Digital Politics Tracker](https://digitalpolitics.co/digital-policy-regulatory-tracker-october-2026)
A quality-management-system standard (EN 18286:2026) is now referenced, giving providers a presumption of compliance. High-risk obligations have been postponed until at least December 2027.

**GPAI enforcement and watermarking** — [CDT Europe bulletin](https://cdt.org/insights/cdt-europes-ai-bulletin-october-2026)
The Commission's consultation on a GPAI enforcement implementing act runs to Nov 9, and OpenAI said it will watermark AI text in the EU with a restricted detection tool, which CDT notes may fall short of the Transparency Code's public-access expectation.

**US federal actions** — [Digital Politics Tracker](https://digitalpolitics.co/digital-policy-regulatory-tracker-october-2026)
DHS moved to remove DeepSeek products from federal civilian procurement (Oct 20), and the FTC ordered nine firms to disclose how algorithmic systems affect individualized pricing and worker pay (Oct 28). No new state AI laws were listed in this tracker; the federal–state preemption fight from the January executive order remains background context.

**UK and others** — [Digital Politics Tracker](https://digitalpolitics.co/digital-policy-regulatory-tracker-october-2026), [BritBrief](https://britbrief.co.uk/tech/regulation/cma-investigates-meta-terms-for-publishers-and-businesses.html)

## 4. Academic Articles

All from arXiv, via a [StartupHub weekly roundup](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/ai-research/2026/ai-research-this-week-autoregressive-to-diffusion-mixture-of-experts-drafting):

**Deduplicating experts and speculative decoding for MoE** — [arXiv 2610.34440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34440v1)
Shows that speedups depend on how often consecutive tokens reuse the same experts, giving a cost model based on memory traffic.

**ThinkBit: two-bit quantization for reasoning models** — [arXiv 2610.34886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34886v1)
Argues perplexity and multiple-choice tests hide reasoning damage from aggressive quantization and proposes a scalar two-bit scheme evaluated on reasoning benchmarks.

**MLE-World** — [arXiv 2610.37175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37175v1)
A small fine-tuned model predicts the outcome of ML code (errors, logs, validation scores) without running it, so agents can prune weak experiments early.

**Lost tool outputs in agent harnesses** — [arXiv 2610.36114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36114v1)
About a third of tool results were truncated before reaching the model in one fault-diagnosis study, so pipeline auditing matters more than context size.

**Build Your Own DLM (BYOD)** — [arXiv 2610.41574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41574v1)
Converts autoregressive LLMs into diffusion language models using only low-rank adapters across four 7–9B models.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: Tokens, cost and open vs. closed models.** Using Mistral Large 4 ($0.70/$2.10) and GPT-6.1 Terra ($1/$5) pricing, students compute cost for a classroom workload, then discuss licensing differences (open weights vs. modified MIT vs. API-only).
2. **Lecture/demo: Quantization and speculative decoding.** Use ThinkBit and the MoE speculation paper to show why perplexity can hide reasoning loss, with a small hands-on comparing a model at 8-bit vs. 4-bit on a few reasoning prompts.
3. **Discussion + mini-lab: AI supply-chain and agent safety.** Use the trojanized extension and the lost-tool-output paper to have students audit an agent pipeline (truncation, permissions, extension provenance) and write a short risk checklist tied to NIST AI RMF; optionally add a Java 27 sidebar on post-quantum TLS and safer defaults.
