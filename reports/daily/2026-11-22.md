## 1. AI industry news

**Leaky AI supply chain exposes user prompts** - [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/a-leaky-ai-supply-chain-is-exposing-secret-user-data-at-an-alarming-rate/) (via [Sam on AI brief](https://samonai.substack.com/p/ai-brief-november-21-2026))
Dragos researcher HD Moore sent about two million prompts seeded with fake credentials to many AI providers. At least three providers appeared to leak or resell the prompts, and the bait cloud keys were later used tens of thousands of times.

**METR Q4 capability update** - [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/)
METR reports models now generally match or beat skilled humans on its coding and optimization tasks but still trail experts where feedback is unclear and judgment matters. It puts the doubling time for the task length models can handle at about 3.6 months.

**AI and the Riemann hypothesis** - [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/)
An Anthropic mathematician posted a Lean-formalized proof attempt developed with Claude, reportedly accepted by independent checkers, while a separate human-written draft is running into problems. A related Navier-Stokes blow-up claim is described as unverified, so expect scrutiny before any of this is accepted.

**AWS agents find and exploit a zero-day in minutes** - [ExplainX summary](https://explainx.ai/blog/amazon-cairn-ai-agents-monai-label-zero-day-root-11-minutes-november-2026)
AWS says its AI agents found and exploited an unpatched upload flaw in MONAI Label on a university medical-imaging server in about 11 minutes. This is a vendor claim reported by an aggregator.

**University of California buys ChatGPT Edu and Codex** - [ExplainX summary](https://explainx.ai/blog/university-of-california-openai-1-2-million-chatgpt-edu-codex-10-campuses-faculty-2026)
UC will reportedly pay about $1.2M a year across 10 campuses, after faculty senate leaders asked for a delay. Directly relevant to campus AI-adoption debates.

**OpenRouter warns about MiniMax routing** - [ExplainX summary](https://explainx.ai/blog/openrouter-minimax-prompts-may-have-been-read-customer-email-november-2026)
OpenRouter told paying users that prompts routed to MiniMax models may have been read and removed zero-data-retention labels from them during an investigation.

**Congress: FRONTIER ACT 2.0 and SAFE AI Act outlines** - [Washington Post doc](https://www.washingtonpost.com/documents/2cc192e0-9af4-40f1-8257-5461bd3ff2fd.pdf), [Rep. Beyer](https://beyer.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=9310)

## 4. Academic articles on AI

**Large Language Models for AI Infrastructure** - [arXiv 2611.19061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19061)
A survey of how LLMs help across the AI infrastructure lifecycle, from chip design and compilers to operations and energy, with a cautious cross-study comparison.

**Large Language Models without QK-Normalization** - [arXiv 2611.10560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10560)
Proposes QK-MAP, which keeps vector magnitude as phase information instead of normalizing it away, and reports lower loss across models from 190M to 7B parameters.

**Large Language Models Have Not Yet Transformed Science** - [arXiv 2611.22975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22975)
An analysis of about 2.5 million papers across five fields finds pre-2023 trends largely continued, with few clear breaks after LLMs arrived.

**Worldviews in Large Language Models** - [arXiv 2611.22670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22670)
Eight LLMs took human worldview surveys and scored the world as less good and safe than humans do; base models were more positive, which the authors tie to post-training.

**Evaluating LLMs for Scientific Paper Search** - [arXiv 2611.17929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17929)
Eight open-weight models were tested on retrieving papers; success depended on model and query specificity, and a Mac Studio's unified memory fit larger models than an A100 despite being slower.

## 5. Java development and writing better code

**JDK 27 feature summary** - [Baeldung](https://www.baeldung.com/java-27-new-features)
JDK 27 (September 2026) has nine JEPs: compact object headers and G1 as the universal default GC are now on by default, TLS 1.3 gets hybrid post-quantum key exchange, and JFR gains sensitive-value redaction. Structured concurrency stays in preview (seventh round) with breaking API changes.

**JDK 27 and security patching** - [Azul](https://www.azul.com/blog/jdk-27-the-quiet-before-the-storm/)
Azul notes a critical security patch delayed the release candidate and argues AI-assisted vulnerability discovery is increasing patch pressure. A banner on that page mentions a further issue affecting several OpenJDK versions that I could not corroborate.

**October Oracle patch round** - [Java Bulletin Digest #110](https://javabulletin.substack.com/p/digest-110-patch-your-jdk-this-week)
The digest reports 55 Java SE fixes in the October update (27.0.1, 25.0.5, 21.0.13 and earlier trains) and reminds readers that Java does not check certificate revocation by default.

**JDK 28 outlook** - same digest and Azul post
General availability is proposed for March 9, 2027; targeted items reportedly include a standard JSON API and value objects, with structured concurrency expected to finalize.

**Spring Boot 4.2 / Framework 7.1 release candidates** - via the digest
Both reached RC1 on Oct 22; RestTemplate is deprecated for removal in favor of RestClient.

**Faster Maven builds** - via the digest (Foojay case study)
Deutsche Bank reportedly roughly halved PR build times using Maven's build cache, with no gain for the first build on a branch.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: Prompt-leak canary experiment.** Using the leaky-AI-supply-chain story, students write a small script that embeds unique fake "canary" tokens in prompts to a local or sandbox model, then discuss how a data-flow audit would detect leakage. Ties to data privacy and vendor risk.
2. **Lecture and discussion: Market reaction to AI hype.** Use the week's sell-off as a case study on concentration risk, the gap between earnings and expectations, and why model-based forecasting of prices is hard. Students plot a provided price series and compare a naive baseline with a simple regression, emphasizing out-of-sample failure.
3. **Lab: Verification versus claims.** Using the Riemann and Navier-Stokes claims and METR's capability update, students evaluate an AI-produced claim with a checklist (reproducibility, formal verification, independent review), then try a tiny Lean or unit-test-based check on a simple AI-written function in Java.
