## 4. Academic AI papers (arXiv)

Summaries below come from an auto-generated digest; claims are the authors' reported results and not independently verified.

**Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm**
Source: [arXiv 2610.40564](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40564v1)
Proposes one RL-style objective to replace separate pretraining, fine-tuning and preference-tuning stages, reportedly shortening the training schedule by about 40%.

**How to Loop Your Transformer**
Source: [arXiv 2610.40325](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40325v1)
Studies reusing the same transformer blocks repeatedly, reportedly doubling parameter efficiency. It connects to discussions of model size versus compute.

**Hessian-Guided Post-Training Quantization**
Source: [arXiv 2610.40345](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40345v1)
A second-order method that reportedly keeps accuracy loss under 1% at 3-bit weights on a 13B model, which matters for running models on modest hardware.

**Energy Accounting of Speculative Decoding**
Source: [arXiv 2610.40287](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40287v1)
Examines energy per generated token under latency targets, reporting about 30% savings. It ties neatly to data-center energy and ESG topics.

**Time-to-event EHR foundation model (TRACER)**
Source: [arXiv 2610.40536](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40536v1)
A decoder-only model for electronic health records reportedly improves early detection of sepsis and heart failure across three health networks.

**Propose-Filter-Release: Private Filtering**
Source: [arXiv 2610.40654](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40654v1)
A differential-privacy technique that scales noise to typical rather than worst-case sensitivity, reportedly cutting needed noise substantially.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: "Is the AI boom paying off?" data analysis.** Give students a small table of capex, cloud revenue growth and stock reaction for Alphabet and Microsoft (from today's coverage). They compute simple ratios, plot them, and write a short argument for each side. This builds data literacy and critical reading of hype.
2. **Lecture and discussion: dual-use AI and agent security.** Use the VAPOR PANDA and AI-coding-tool vulnerability stories to introduce agents, tool use and prompt injection. Students then red-team a toy agent in a sandbox, listing what could go wrong and which controls (least privilege, logging, human approval) would help.
3. **Lab: efficiency tricks for running models.** Using the quantization and speculative-decoding papers as context, students compare a small open model at different precision levels (for example 4-bit versus 8-bit) on speed, memory and answer quality, then relate energy per token to the data-center cost debate. A Java extension is calling a local model from LangChain4j and timing responses.
