# Daily AI & Java Research Digest - 2026-10-26

Prepared for CISC 121 Introduction to AI and Machine Learning. Sources were reached via web search; several are secondary aggregators, so verify key figures against primary sources before citing them in class.

## Top takeaways

- Frontier-lab pricing keeps collapsing: GPT-6.1 Sol launched at roughly one-fifth of GPT-6 Astra's price, and one analyst estimates a cumulative drop of about 75% across the OpenAI model family since late summer.
- Google's Gemini 4 Argon is being released first to vetted cyber defenders, and AI-found vulnerabilities are in the news (two decade-old PHP flaws; 38 Apache Camel CVEs, most found with Claude).
- Regulation is diverging: the EU is delaying high-risk rules through an omnibus bill, the US relies on voluntary accords and executive orders, and California signed SB 947 limiting automated firing.
- JDK 27 is out (G1 default, compact object headers), and JDK 28 is lining up Structured Concurrency for finalization after seven previews.
- Markets are jittery: semiconductors fell sharply, Nasdaq led losses, oil is above $100, and a Fed meeting plus mega-cap earnings land this week. Informational only, not financial advice.

## 1. AI industry news

**Anthropic IPO filing details** - [DEV Community daily digest, Oct 24](https://dev.to/hiroki-ii-ai/ai-daily-digest-october-24-2026-anthropic-opens-the-books-ai-finds-phps-oldest-bugs-europe-118c)
Reported draft figures show first-half 2026 revenue of about $16.3B and large long-term cloud commitments. Valuation talk runs from $1.5T to $2T, and OpenAI is reportedly arranging a separate private round. These are reported figures from a secondary source, not confirmed filings.

**OpenAI Decisions API reaches general availability** - [DEV Community daily digest, Oct 24](https://dev.to/hiroki-ii-ai/ai-daily-digest-october-24-2026-anthropic-opens-the-books-ai-finds-phps-oldest-bugs-europe-118c)
Instead of generating text, the endpoint returns a probability, choice, or score, which makes it suited to classification-style agent decisions. OpenAI claims large speed gains over using the general Responses API. It is a good concrete example of the shift from generation to decision scoring.

**Two decade-old PHP vulnerabilities found by AI-driven research** - [DEV Community daily digest, Oct 24](https://dev.to/hiroki-ii-ai/ai-daily-digest-october-24-2026-anthropic-opens-the-books-ai-finds-phps-oldest-bugs-europe-118c)
The flaws affect PHP versions back to 2004, and details leaked before a planned fix, prompting some government sites to go offline as a precaution. Patched releases are available. The case shows both the defensive promise and the disclosure risks of AI-assisted bug hunting.

**Why LLM prices keep falling** - [minimaxir, "Cheap LLMs"](https://minimaxir.com/2026/10/cheap-llms)
The author argues that competition and interchangeable open-weight models, not temporary subsidies, are driving cuts. Cache-read pricing matters most for agentic coding because it dominates token spend. Per-token cost fell while total spending rose as usage grew faster. The IPO-motive commentary is the author's speculation.

**Wayve, Stellantis and NVIDIA partner on autonomous driving in Europe** - [DEV Community daily digest, Oct 24](https://dev.to/hiroki-ii-ai/ai-daily-digest-october-24-2026-anthropic-opens-the-books-ai-finds-phps-oldest-bugs-europe-118c)
The partnership aims to deploy self-driving vehicles in France and then scale across the EU. The EU approval route for fully driverless cars is capped at a small annual volume. A useful applied-ML story about hardware, models, and regulation interacting.

## 2. AI regulation and policy

**Three regulatory models compared: EU, US, China** - [Euronews, Oct 7](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/07/laws-pledges-or-an-iron-grip-how-the-eu-us-and-china-are-reining-in-ai)
The EU uses binding risk tiers but has accepted delays for high-risk rules. The US leans on voluntary commitments, including a September 29 White House accord among six AI companies that promises audits without penalties. China regulates content and companion chatbots under state control. Congress has passed no federal AI law and is out until after the midterms.

**California SB 947 on automated employment decisions** - [AI Agents news brief digest, Oct 24](https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today/edition/2026-10-01)
The law bars employers from firing or disciplining workers based solely on automated systems and takes effect July 1, 2027, according to the digest. It is a concrete example of state-level human-in-the-loop rules. Check the bill text for exact scope.

**Global tracker snapshot of AI laws** - [Mean CEO AI regulation page](https://blog.mean.ceo/ai-regulation-news-october-2026/)
A tracker cited there counts more than 1,100 AI instruments across over 100 jurisdictions, with most already in force. The page also recaps EU penalty tiers and the federal push against state AI laws. Its status notes on Colorado and EU deadlines are stale, so confirm with primary sources.

**Federal preemption debate continues** - [Holland & Knight on the White House framework](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/white-house-releases-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial)
Law-firm analysis describes the White House national framework and the fight over preempting state AI laws. This is background from March, not new this week, and I could not retrieve the October Federal News Network piece on lawmakers' next steps.

## 4. Academic AI articles (arXiv, new this week)

**A Fix for Softmax and Cross-Entropy** - [arXiv 2610.31642](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31642v1)
Addresses numerical problems in common softmax and cross-entropy code under extreme logit values. A natural supplement to any lecture on classification loss.

**Direct Pass@k Optimization for Thought Pruning** - [arXiv 2610.31667](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31667v1)
Trims redundant reasoning paths at inference time to cut cost while keeping answer quality and diversity.

**Causal Chain of Thought** - [arXiv 2610.31639](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31639v1)
Uses context isolation so each reasoning step genuinely affects the final answer rather than serving as an after-the-fact story.

**EpiSEM-Bench** - [arXiv 2610.31650](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31650v1)
A benchmark testing whether tool-using agents avoid unsafe actions when tool outputs omit safety-relevant facts.

**Reliable AI-Assisted Decisions via Selective Auditing** - [arXiv 2610.31662](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31662v1)
Shows how auditing only a subset of confidence-scored AI decisions can still maintain reliability, relevant to human oversight design.

## Teaching ideas for CISC 121

1. **Lab: Softmax stability.** Have students implement naive softmax and cross-entropy in Python, feed extreme logits to see overflow and NaNs, then fix it with the log-sum-exp trick. Tie it to the new arXiv paper on numerical bugs.
2. **Lecture and discussion: Why AI prices fall.** Use the LLM price-cut timeline to compare cost per token with total spend, and have students build a small spreadsheet model of an agentic app's monthly cost under different cache-read prices.
3. **Lab: Human-in-the-loop decision scoring in Java.** Students write a small Java program using JDK 27/28 features (records, structured concurrency preview) that scores items with a mock classifier, audits a random subset as in the selective-auditing paper, and reports error rates. Discuss it against California SB 947 and the EU risk tiers.
